      Make a figure from photos

      Photos of a toy or figure from the front, its left side and the back (the right side is optional) become a coloured 3D model, made on this computer by Pixal3D in ComfyUI. Plain background, the whole figure in each photo, camera at its middle height. It takes a few minutes.

      Ordinary photos are first redrawn as clean front, side and back views by Qwen-Image-Edit (about 20 GB more weights; see AGENTS.md), then made into a model as above.

      One photo works too (a single-view model, turned to face front when its shape makes that clear; the Figure card turns it by hand). Three straight views give a better back and sides, and the photos' own colours.

      Generation options

      Pixal3D's settings. Higher detail costs time; another seed gives another plausible shape from the same photos.

      pokefit — fit any figure inside a Pokéball, with magnets

      pokefit takes a 3D-printable figure (3MF, STL or OBJ), scales it to the largest size that fits inside a printed Pokéball, cuts pockets for magnets into its underside so it snaps onto a steel washer glued in the ball, checks that it will print, and exports a ready-to-slice Bambu Studio 3MF with its painted colours.

      How to use it

      1. Open a figure — drop a .3mf, .stl, .obj or .glb on the page. Files with several figures get a picker. A .glb keeps its colours (texture or materials) as filaments; choose how many under Colours.
      2. Pick the ball — the 60 % or 100 % ball, or type the inner diameter of yours. Set the clearance to the walls.
      3. Pick the washer — plain or zinc-plated steel (stainless is not magnetic). Compare washers shows which allows the largest figure.
      4. Magnets — 1, 2 or 3 pockets for 4 × 2 mm disc magnets are placed automatically; drag them in the underside view. By default every magnet rests on the washer; under a part that floats above the bed (a tail) the magnet shows below it. Choose Raised into the figure to hide it in the part instead (it then holds less). Nothing goes below the plane the figure stands on.
      5. Colour it — for single-colour models: pick a colour, press Paint and tap the figure. Each tap fills a part found from the shape (up to the valley where an ear, tail, leg or eye meets the body), a separate piece, or a brush patch. Show regions previews the parts. Exported as Bambu paint.
      6. Shape it for the ball (Figure card) — legs or a tail lying straight out past the washer are bent up along the sphere so the figure fits larger, and the base over the washer is made flat (low bumps pressed, gaps under the body filled with a solid flat base) so the magnets have room. Set the bend to Off and the sizes to 0 to keep the model exactly as it is.
      7. Turn it — rotate about X, Y and Z by any angle (45° works) if the model is not standing upright.
      8. Export — the figure 3MF (opens as a Bambu Studio project), an import variant whose colours are renumbered to your own project's filament slots (for Import geometry only), and a JSON report. Project or presets… also reads Bambu Studio's preset exports (File › Export › Export Preset Bundle: .bbscfg, .bbsflmt, presets .zip): their filaments become materials to print with, their process presets selectable settings.

      What it checks

      • Fit against the ball's inner sphere, the connector rings and the ring opening the figure must pass through when closing.
      • Mesh soundness: closed surface, consistent normals, degenerate faces, separate shells.
      • Wall thickness, overhangs that need support, layers that start in mid-air, and bed contact (brim advice).
      • Magnet pockets: wall around each pocket, material above it, how high it sits above the washer, and whether it is over the steel.

      Privacy

      Everything runs in your browser: the same Python engine as the desktop version, compiled to WebAssembly (Pyodide). Your files are never uploaded. The first visit downloads about 30 MB, which the browser then caches.

      Local version (print time and filament)

      Print time, filament use and the supports verdict come from Bambu Studio itself, which only runs on your computer. The local version slices with your own printer profile:

      git clone https://github.com/tribixbite/pokefit
cd pokefit && pip install -e .
cd web && bun install && bun run build && cd ..
python -m pokefit serve --open

      With an NVIDIA GPU (16 GB) and ComfyUI running Pixal3D, the local version also makes a figure from photos (front, left side, back): a coloured model in a few minutes, ready to fit. Setup: AGENTS.md in the repository, "Photos to 3D".

      Questions

      Are my files uploaded anywhere?
      No. The analysis runs in your browser (Python compiled to WebAssembly); files never leave your device.
      Which washer and magnets should I use?
      A plain or zinc-plated steel washer (stainless is not magnetic), glued inside the ball's bottom, and 4 × 2 mm disc magnets pressed into the pockets. A 1/4 inch × 1 inch fender washer or a DIN 9021 M6 washer works for the 60 % ball; Compare washers shows which gives the largest figure.
      Which file types work?
      3MF (including Bambu Studio projects with painted colours and several figures), STL and OBJ.
      How do I get print time and filament estimates?
      Those come from Bambu Studio itself, which runs only on your computer: install the local version of pokefit (Python) to slice with your own printer settings.
